# Section-splitting benchmark

This benchmark measures how well `extract_sections` finds the Items of 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, 10-KSB and 10-QSB filings, and how long text conversion and splitting take. It compares the output with hand labels for 234 filings filed from 1996 to 2026, in seven sets. The same labels also score [edgar-crawler](https://github.com/nlpaueb/edgar-crawler) and [edgartools](https://github.com/dgunning/edgartools).

## Accuracy in brief

The numbers that show how the extractor does on filings it has not seen are each set's first scoring, made before any of that set's misses were fixed. The newest set, fresh4, was labeled and committed before any scoring, then scored once with changes 1 to 49 below. That scoring, F1 0.998 with span accuracy 0.983, is its one blind score, and it stays the record. Changes 50 to 52 came after it. They fix layouts found with made-up filings and change no fresh4 filing. Changes 53 to 61 came later, and with them fresh4 is in-sample: changes 56 to 59 were written from its misses, and two of its labels were anchored on other lines (change 55). It now scores 1.000 on every measure, which is not evidence for filings the extractor has not seen. The extractor has now been tuned on all seven sets, so every current score is in-sample. Changes 56 to 61 were checked out of sample another way: they were run on 770 filings that are in no label set, and every filing whose sections or titles changed was read (see [Regression check on unlabeled filings](#regression-check-on-unlabeled-filings)). Those filings were read, not labeled, so that review gives no precision or recall. Measuring later rules out of sample will take another new set, labeled and committed before it is scored.

At its first scoring fresh4 had precision 1.000 and recall 0.995: 438 of the 440 labeled Item starts were found, and no predicted Item starts on another line. Five filings lost span, two of them a quarter or more of it: a 10-Q whose Part I Item 1 is printed only as sub-headings "Item 1. a." to "Item 1. e." and was missed (Bridgford Foods 2007, span 0.699), and a 10-K whose Item 15 ran on through a schedule and an investee's statements printed after it (Donegal Group 2018, span 0.746). All five have since been fixed (changes 56 to 59), which is why the set's current score is in-sample. Changes 37 to 49 made no difference on this set: main before them scores the same on every filing. Version 0.1.1 cannot read fresh4's two 10-KSB and two 10-QSB filings; on the other 30 it scores F1 0.996 with span 0.953, against 0.999 and 0.981 at the first scoring, and no filing was worse at the first scoring. edgar-crawler scores F1 0.984 with span 0.937, and edgartools F1 0.770 with span 0.554 (see [Scores](#scores)). On fresh3 at its first scoring, edgar-crawler's F1 was higher than parsec-edgar's (0.992 against 0.984), because of one cross-reference 10-K, and on fresh2 its span accuracy was (0.917 against 0.895). Compare the other tools with the first scorings, not with the current in-sample scores of the older sets.

Span accuracy is computed per filing: the characters assigned to the right Item, divided by the characters inside any labeled or predicted Item. The tables show the mean over filings. It is not the share of labeled text that was found. A filing whose last Item runs on through pages that the labels leave out of every Item scores well under 1, even when every Item starts on the right line.

## Running it

```sh
export PARSEC_EDGAR_USER_AGENT="Your Name you@example.com"
cargo run --release -p parsec-edgar-bench -- score                           # development set
cargo run --release -p parsec-edgar-bench -- --labels bench/holdout score
cargo run --release -p parsec-edgar-bench -- --labels bench/blind score
cargo run --release -p parsec-edgar-bench -- --labels bench/fresh score
cargo run --release -p parsec-edgar-bench -- --labels bench/fresh2 score
cargo run --release -p parsec-edgar-bench -- --labels bench/fresh3 score
cargo run --release -p parsec-edgar-bench -- --labels bench/fresh4 score
```

The first run downloads the labeled documents into the cache, at 3 requests per second. The cache is `~/.cache/parsec-edgar`, or `PARSEC_EDGAR_CACHE` when set. Later runs read only the cache.

`score` takes these options:
- `--filter TEXT` keeps only filings whose accession number, company or form contains TEXT.
- `--repeat N` times each filing N times and keeps the fastest run.
- `--json FILE` writes the per-filing results.
- `--no-misses` leaves out the list of misses.

The report has one row per group: 10-K by era of the filing date, 10-KSB, 10-QSB, 10-Q, 8-K, all 10-Ks (without the 10-KSBs), and all filings. A group with no filings in the set is left out. It then has one row per tag. After that it lists every filing with a miss, Item by Item, with the labeled and predicted line numbers and character counts.

## What is measured

- **Item start.** A predicted Item is a hit when it starts on the labeled heading line.
  - Precision is hits divided by predicted Items, recall is hits divided by labeled Items, and F1 is their harmonic mean.
  - A predicted Item that is not labeled counts against precision, for example an Item that a filing lists only in its table of contents.
- **Span accuracy.** For each filing: the characters assigned to the right Item, divided by the characters inside any labeled or predicted Item. The table shows the mean over filings. This catches Items that start right but end wrong, for example a last Item that runs on into an attached annual report.
- **Errors.** A filing whose document cannot be read, or whose labels no longer resolve in the text, stays in every row: all its labeled Items count as missed and its span accuracy as 0. The report lists these filings under "Errors".
- **Warnings.** A label that finds its line only through a fallback, because the line after it no longer starts with the label's `next`, is listed after the errors, and each filing's JSON result has such labels under `warnings`. The filing is scored as usual (change 55).
- **Time.** All times are from a release build.
  - Parse is `document_to_text` and Split is `extract_sections`. Both columns show the median per filing in milliseconds.
  - Total is the sum over the group's filings.
  - The document is read from the cache before timing starts.

## Labels

There is one JSON file per filing, named by accession number. It contains:
- the filing's metadata;
- the URL of the document that was read (the primary document, or the complete submission for filings without one);
- `tags`;
- `items`: one entry per Item that has a heading in the body, in document order.

Each Item entry has these fields:
- `text`: the heading line as printed.
- `occurrence`: the line's index among lines with the same normalized text.
- `next`: the start of the following line.
- `incorporated`: optional. The Item only refers to another document or says "not applicable".
- `uncertain`: optional. The label itself is uncertain.
- `end`: optional. Where the Item ends, when that is not the next labeled heading. It is used for text that belongs to no Item, such as an executive officers section numbered "Item 4A", or financial statements printed with no Item heading.

At the filing level:
- `absent` lists Items the form defines but the filing has no heading for.
- `end` marks where the last Item ends: the signatures, an exhibit index, or an attached report.

An Item runs from its heading to the next labeled heading, or to `end`. Labels are found again by their text, not by line number, so they survive changes to the text conversion.

The labels were made without the extractor's output:
- `items ACCESSION` lists every line that looks like an Item, Part, signatures or exhibit-index heading, using a pattern of its own. It shows each line with the text that follows it, including contents entries and page headers.
- `lines ACCESSION [--from N --to M --grep RE]` prints numbered lines.
- `set ACCESSION 1:120 1A:340 7:900 ... --end 2400` checks that the chosen lines resolve, writes them to the label file and prints them back for checking. A file is written only when all its labels resolve.
- `add TICKER ACCESSION --tag ...` creates a label file for a new filing. It reads the complete submission only when the filing has no primary document (the SEC answers 404); any other download failure stops it.

### The fresh2 set

**How it was chosen.** A script chose the 30 filings from EDGAR's quarterly master indexes, before any labeling. The set is a fixed list of slots, each naming a form type, a filing year, an industry and a size class. A slot with no industry noted is for a company that is neither a bank nor an insurer, and a slot with no size noted is for a large one:
- 10-K: 1997 (bank), 1998 (10-K405, small), 2000, 2002, 2004 (insurer), 2006 (small), 2008, 2009, 2011 (bank, small), 2013, 2015, 2016, 2018 (insurer), 2020 (small), 2021, 2023 (bank), 2024 (small), 2026.
- 10-Q: 1997, 2002 (bank, small), 2007, 2011, 2016, 2021, 2026.
- 8-K: 2005, 2010, 2014, 2020 (small), 2025.

A slot's candidates are all filings of exactly its form type in the four quarterly indexes of its year (for 2026 the first three, since the fourth was still open), so amendments are never candidates. They are sorted by the SHA-256 hex digest of "fresh2:" followed by the accession number ("fresh2:0000928385-97-000562"), and the slot takes the first candidate that passes these tests, checked in this order. Slots are filled in the order listed above.
1. No company that the index lists for the filing is in another label set or in the regression sample below, or was chosen for an earlier slot.
2. The accession number starts with the company's own CIK, or its prefix has fewer than two filings in the set so far, so no filing agent has more than two.
3. The company is not a trust or an asset-backed issuer: its SIC code is not 6189, and its name holds neither the word Trust (banks excepted) nor a securitization term (receivables, pass-through, certificates, mortgage loan, asset-backed, grantor, securitization).
4. EDGAR has an SIC code for the company, and it fits the slot: banks are 6020 to 6036, insurers 6311 to 6399, and every other slot excludes both ranges.
5. The public float, the market value of common equity held by non-affiliates, fits the slot's size: under $75 million (the accelerated-filer threshold) for a small company, and $75 million or more for a large one. It is read from the filing's own cover for a 10-K, and from the cover of the company's latest 10-K or 10-K405 filed on or before it for a 10-Q or 8-K. A candidate is skipped when that cover states no float: no trading market, a wholly owned subsidiary, partnership units, $0 held by non-affiliates, or no earlier 10-K.
6. Lines 1 to 40 of the benchmark's text of the document are readable text.
7. For an 8-K, the `items` command shows at least one "Item N.NN" heading.

292 candidates were skipped: 185 for their industry, 40 as trusts or asset-backed issuers by name, 9 for SIC code 6189, 20 for falling on the wrong side of $75 million, 18 for stating no float, 10 for being in an existing label set and 10 for the filing-agent cap. None was skipped because its document could not be read. Company names and SIC codes are EDGAR's current values, so a few names differ from the filer's name at the time.

- In ITT's 10-K 2015, one labeler ended Item 15 at the index to the financial statements printed after it, and the other ran it on through the statements. Statements with no Item heading belong to no Item, as in the Netflix 2003 label, so Item 15 ends at the index.

Changes 22 to 36 came after it too, and were made from the five older sets and the regression sample before fresh3's first scoring; [The fresh3 set](#the-fresh3-set) says which of them came before its labels were committed. Changes 37 to 49 came after fresh3's first scoring, with its misses in view, and were made from the six older sets and the regression sample before fresh4's first scoring. Each of them was reviewed with made-up filings near its rule, and the entries say what the review changed. Changes 50 to 52 came after fresh4's first scoring and fix three layouts that changes 37, 39 and 49 had broken, found with made-up filings; they change no benchmark or sample filing. Version 0.2.0 has changes 1 to 52. Changes 53 to 61 came after it. Changes 53 to 55 are about the text and the benchmark's labels and change no score. Changes 56 to 59 were made from the misses of fresh4's first scoring and from the one miss left in fresh3, so both sets' scores are in-sample from change 56 on. Changes 60 and 61 change titles only. Changes 56 to 61 were also run on filings in no label set, and the entries say what changed there.

1. **Plain-text filings.** Old filings whose text contains SGML `<TABLE>` tags are no longer rendered as HTML. Rendering them as HTML merged their lines into a few long ones.
2. **Contents entries.** A table-of-contents entry is dropped when the same Item and title come back later at a longer stretch of text. Entries next to a dropped one go with it.
   - After version 0.1.1: the spread to neighboring entries is narrower. An entry goes when the candidate before it is a contents entry too, or when it comes before the first body heading that a contents entry names and no Item earlier in the form's order, with text of its own, comes before it. A short body Item printed once just before a second contents list, or before an index of Items, stays. An Item line above the contents (a 10-Q/A's explanatory note) no longer keeps the contents rows after it.
3. **Running headers.** A bare running header ("Item 7") just before the Item's titled heading is dropped. A sub-item reference such as "Item 15(a)" with no title is not a heading.
4. **End of the last Item.** The last Item ends:
   - at the signatures;
   - at the signature block's opening sentence, when there is no signatures heading;
   - at an attached document with its own page-numbered table of contents.
5. **Page headers and footers.** Lines that carry a page number and repeat page after page are removed from the text.
   - After version 0.1.1: short headers and footers that name the form ("2021 Form 10-K 100") or start with "Page N of M" are removed too, also when the form name is spelled with a non-breaking hyphen or an en dash. So are footers printed as a three-cell row with the page number in the middle cell, and short footers printed cell by cell that repeat on five or more pages with only a rising page number changing. A page numbered in roman numerals followed by one in digits counts as rising. Lines that are the cells of a table row with two or more cells are never removed one by one, so table values such as "5" or "2024" stay.
6. **10-Q parts.** PART lines decide whether a 10-Q Item belongs to Part I or Part II. Items printed out of order are kept.
   - After version 0.1.1: an Item printed after a higher-numbered Item with no PART line above it is added out of order when its title is the standard title of that Item in Part I only. Leading words "Consolidated", "Condensed", "Unaudited" and "Interim" do not stop that match, so "Item 1. Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements" counts as Part I. Part II titles are not used, because Part II Items 1A and 4 share number and title with 10-K Items that 10-Qs cite. An out-of-order Item can also come before the chain's first heading, when it is at or after the first body heading that a contents entry names (MD&A printed before longer financial statements).
7. **10-K Items the form does not define.** A titled heading such as "Item 4A. Executive Officers" ends the Item before it.
   - After version 0.1.1: an executive officers caption numbered as a sub-item ("ITEM 4(a). EXECUTIVE OFFICERS OF THE REGISTRANT") reads as Item 4A in a 10-K when the form does not define that Item, so it ends Item 4 the same way. "Item 4A" in a 10-K never starts Item 4, whatever its title. When an Item is printed only as lettered headings the form does not define ("Item 5A", "Item 5B"), the first one placed where that Item belongs starts it, if no heading of that Item was found; the later ones stay inside it. In a 10-Q, such a heading takes its part from a PART line or its title.
8. **Item lists after the signatures.** A list of Item headings printed again after the signatures is ignored.
9. **Review fixes.** A code review found layouts where changes 2, 3 and 8 lose Items that the initial commit found, and two layouts that change 6 gets wrong. Unit tests written for layouts near each rule found two more cases. Each case now has a unit test that fails without its fix:
   - A "Signatures" line inside the table of contents no longer makes the body's headings look like a list printed after the signatures. Only a signatures line at or after the first body heading counts, so an empty "Item 6. [Reserved]" and a short last Item are kept.
   - Contents entries spread to their neighbors, but not onto the later copy of a heading that appears twice, so a short body Item ("Not applicable.", "Item 6. [Reserved]") just before a second contents list is kept.
   - A bare heading ("ITEM 1." with "BUSINESS" on the next line) is kept when its next line is the title of the heading that follows it, or the Item's standard title. Only bare running headers are dropped.
   - The search for a PART line above a 10-Q heading stops at another Item's heading line, including a contents row such as "PART II - Item 6. Exhibits", so a "PART II" in the contents no longer moves the body's first Items to Part II.
   - Out-of-order 10-Q Items are added only before the signatures.
   - A 10-Q Item with no PART line above it takes its part from its title, when the title is the standard title of that Item in only one part. A lone "Item 1. Legal Proceedings" is then Part II.
   - A letter joins the Item number when spaces alone separate them ("Item 9 A.") or when a separator with no space after it does ("Item 1.A."), so "ITEM 1. A. GENERAL" stays Item 1.
10. **Dashes in headings** (after version 0.1.1). Dashes right after the separator ("Item 3. - Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures", "ITEM 1 -- FINANCIAL STATEMENTS") are skipped before the title test, in 10-K, 10-Q and 8-K headings, so the text after them must still look like a title. A run of dashes between PART and ITEM ("PART I -- ITEM 2") is accepted too.
11. **Text with no Item heading** (after version 0.1.1). A line that opens such text ends 10-K Items 9 and 16, and Item 7A when the filing has no Item 8 heading: an auditors' report, an index to the financial statements, a financial statement schedule ("SCHEDULE I - CONDENSED PARENT COMPANY FINANCIAL STATEMENTS"), an exhibit index or an accountants' consent. The line must start with a capital letter. Other Items keep these headings as their own text. An Item 16 titled as the exhibits Item keeps its statements and exhibit index, and the rule does not cut a section that also holds the next Item ("Items 9 and 9A.", or "ITEM 9 A – CONTROLS AND PROCEDURES" after "ITEM 9.").
12. **Wrapped cross-references** (after version 0.1.1). In a 10-Q, a line that starts with "Item" and a Part I title but runs on as a sentence ("Item 1. Financial Statements, of this report", "Item 2. Management's Discussion and Analysis, under ...") is a hard-wrapped cross-reference, not a heading, and no longer cuts the Item it sits in short. Only a reference word such as "of this report", or a comma followed by a lowercase word, makes it one; a heading that goes on with words of its own stays.
13. **Form names without a hyphen** (after version 0.1.1). `FormKind::from_form` accepts EDGAR's index spellings 10KSB, 10KSB40, 10KT405, 10QSB and the like, so 10-KSB and 10-QSB filings are split. The four 10-KSB filings of the development set use the spelling "10KSB".
14. **Table cells in the text** (after version 0.1.1). A "$" or "(" cell joins the cell after it only when that cell starts with a digit, a dash or a ".", a "%" cell joins the cell before it only when that cell ends with a digit or a dash, and a ")" cell joins a cell that leaves a "(" open. Header cells such as "Amount" keep the "%" column beside them as a cell of their own, and "$" next to "N/A" stays apart. The same merges now happen in rows printed cell by cell. These change only the text inside Items.
15. **10-KSB and 10-QSB Items** (#14). 10-KSB and 10-QSB filings are split with Item lists of their own, with the titles of the forms' last versions (2008), instead of the 10-K and 10-Q lists. The 10-KSB list has Items 8A and 8B, and also Item 1A (risk factors) and Item 15 (accountant fees, printed in 10-KSBs for 2003, whose Item 14 was controls and procedures). The 10-QSB list has Part I Items 1 to 3 and Part II Items 1, 1A and 2 to 6. The 10-K rules for the executive officers caption and for titled Items the form does not define apply to a 10-KSB, and the 10-Q rules for parts apply to a 10-QSB, with its own titles. A block heading (change 11) ends 10-KSB Item 8 (changes in accountants) and Items 14 and 15, which come after the exhibits, unless the Item is titled as the exhibits Item.
    - A filing is split with the other form's list (10-K and 10-KSB, 10-Q and 10-QSB) when its headings fit that list strictly better. The count is the Items printed with a number and a title that one list gives to that number alone and the other list gives to another number and not to this one. So "Item 7. Financial Statements" counts for 10-KSB and "Item 8. Financial Statements" for 10-K, while "Item 4. Submission of Matters to a Vote of Security Holders" and Items 14 and 15 "Principal Accountant Fees and Services" count for neither. A tie keeps the form type's list.
16. **Lettered sub-items and form names in titles.** A sub-item printed with the standard title of a lettered Item that the form defines is that Item: "Item 8(A)T. Controls and Procedures" in a 10-KSB is Item 8A, and "ITEM 9(A). CONTROLS AND PROCEDURES" in a 10-K is Item 9A. This holds also when nothing follows the letter and the title is on the next line ("ITEM 9(A)." or "Item 9(a)." above "CONTROLS AND PROCEDURES"). Another title ("ITEM 1(a). GENERAL DEVELOPMENT OF BUSINESS") or a sub-item reference ("Item 9(a)(1)") stays inside the Item. A title may also start with a form's name followed by a capitalized word, so "ITEM 16. 10-K SUMMARY" is a heading, while "Item 16 10-K filed in 1998" is not.
17. **Financial statements after the exhibits Item** (#12). The exhibits Item (10-K Item 15, the old Item 14, 10-KSB Item 13) ends where it prints financial statements whose pages are numbered apart from the report's, after all of its own text. That needs an index of at least three entries with F-page numbers ("F-2", "F - 1", "F-7 to F-20"), then headings with the titles of at least two of its entries. The Item ends at the index's heading when the statements follow the index directly, and otherwise at the first statement, so an index that is the Item's own "(a)(1)" list stays in it. Statements with the report's own page numbers stay in the Item under this rule; change 33 ends the last exhibits Item at them when the statements Item only refers to them. Statements printed before another Item stay, as NVIDIA's 2025 labels have them, and 10-K Item 8 and 10-KSB Item 7 are never cut this way.
    - The statements stay in the Item when any of its own text follows the first of them: an exhibit index, a caption such as "(a)(3) Exhibits:", "3. Exhibits", "LIST OF EXHIBITS", "2. Financial Statement Schedules" or "(b) Reports on Form 8-K", or an exhibit table's header ("Exhibit No.", "Exhibit Number"). Change 33 added more captions and headers. Here an exhibit index still keeps the statements in the Item; under change 33 it does not. Numbered notes inside the statements ("2. Inventories", "(b) Use of estimates") do not count.
    - An Item is the exhibits Item by its printed title only when it comes after the Item that holds the statements (10-K Item 8, 10-KSB Item 7), so a 10-K's Item 7 printed as "Financial Statements" keeps its statements.
18. **Glossaries after the last Item** (#13). A glossary heading ("Glossary", "GLOSSARY OF SELECTED TERMS:", "Glossary of Terms and Acronyms") ends 10-K Item 16 and 10-KSB Items 14 and 15, as a block heading does, unless the Item is titled as the exhibits Item. The line must start with a capital letter. A contents entry ("Glossary" and a page number) or a sentence is not a heading, and a glossary inside another Item stays there.
19. **Wrapped cross-references in every form** (#15). An Item line is not a heading, in any form, when the line just above it ends inside a sentence: with a comma, a joining word ("in", "of", "to", "and" and the like), or "see", "under", "within" or "this". For example, "The information set forth in Part II," then "Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis, of the Company's Annual Report ...". A blank line, or a line holding only a PART label ("PART II,"), does not count. The words are compared in their case, so a page header in capitals that ends in "AND" does not count either.
20. **Page labels** (#16). Page numbers alone on their line after a dash and a prefix of one to three capital letters ("FS - 12", "S-3", "II-4"), a chapter number ("7-3") or nothing ("- 4") are removed when five or more copies of one prefix are mostly not on consecutive lines and mostly come one to three pages after the copy before, or start a new chapter at page 3 or lower. So exhibit lists ("EX-21", "EX-23") and number ranges stay. "F - 3" with any dash and spacing, "- -7-" in old text filings and numbered "<Page 2>" tags are removed as page numbers. A "Page 1 of M" footer after any page counts as rising, as when each exhibit numbers its own pages, unless the page is past the count. Running headers numbered in lowercase roman numerals join their family. A short header that does not name the form is removed when a "|" separates the number from it ("ACME | 23"), or when the number is at the start of some copies and at the end of others, at least two of each, as on facing pages. These change no score.
21. **Headings joined by "and"** (after the first scoring of fresh2). A line that holds only a joining word, such as "and" between "Item 1. Business." and "Item 2. Properties.", links the two headings, so change 19 no longer drops the heading after it.
22. **"Item 9 A (T)."** (#21). A letter printed apart from the Item number also joins it when "(T)" follows the letter, so "Item 9 A (T). Controls and Procedures." is Item 9A, as "Item 9A(T)" already was. Before, it read as a second Item 9 titled "A (T). Controls ...". "ITEM 9 A – CONTROLS", with a dash after the letter, still reads as Item 9.
23. **Comma after the Item number** (#22). "Item 8, Financial Statements and Supplementary Data" is a heading when the line ends where the Item's current standard title ends. Words are compared whole, without joining words, so "Item 8, which ...", a page number, or a shortened, longer or earlier title fails. A list of Items with commas ("ITEMS 9, 9A AND 9B.", "ITEMS 10., 11., 12. AND 13.", "ITEMS 1., 1A. and 2. BUSINESS, RISK FACTORS AND PROPERTIES") is a heading when its Items go up in form order within one part, "and" or "&" comes before the last one, and nothing or a capitalized title follows the numbers ("Items 2, 3 and 4. Not applicable."). Like "Items 1 and 2.", it starts the first Item, and its section holds the others. Lines that go on as a sentence ("Items 3, 4 and 5 are not applicable") and running headers without "and" ("Item 9, 9A") are not headings, and change 19 still applies. In the first version, the text after a list had to be the standard titles of all of its Items; the review gave it the test of a dash range, so a list followed by "Not applicable." counts as the dash and "and" forms of the same line do.
24. **Ranges with a dash** (#20). "Item 1-5.", "ITEMS 10 - 13." and "ITEMS 10-14.", with a hyphen, an en dash or an em dash, start the first Item of the range, as "Items 1 through 5." does. The range must go up in form order within one part, and the rest of the line must be empty or start like a title. "Item 1-2 of the Form", "Item 2-1.", "Item 7 - 2005 ...", "Item 5 - 10,000 shares", the contents row "Item 1. - 6.   12" and "ITEM 16 - 10-K SUMMARY" are not ranges.
25. **Lines that only refer to several Items** (#22, #20, from the review). Changes 23 and 24 made more lines that name several Items into candidates, and when the first Item's body heading was far away, such a line won: "Part II – Items 7, 8 and 9A" in a 10-K/A's explanatory note started Item 7 there. The "and" form ("Part II – Items 7 and 8") did the same before this round. A line that names several Items, in any of the forms above or with "and" or "through", with or without titles, is now dropped when a heading of its first Item alone follows it before any heading of an Item the line does not name. A line that a contents entry names stays, and so does a heading of several Items that the heading of one of the others follows ("Item 7A." inside "ITEMS 7 AND 7A."). A comma list or a dash range with nothing after the numbers, on a line that starts with a Part ("Part III – Items 10, 11, 12, 13 and 14"), is no candidate at all.
    - Cover rows (43ae3a5, before fresh3's first scoring). A cover's list of documents incorporated by reference can print "Items 10, 11, 12, 13 and 14" or "Items 10 - 13" without a Part. In a 10-K that prints no Item headings in its body, that row was the only candidate, so Item 10 ran from the cover to the signatures; before changes 23 and 24 the filing had no sections. In a 10-K or 10-KSB, a comma list or dash range with nothing after the numbers that does not name Item 1 is now dropped when only such lines come before it, unless the heading of another Item it names comes next ("ITEMS 9, 9A AND 9B." then "Item 9A."). Body headings of several later Items ("ITEMS 10., 11., 12. AND 13.", "ITEMS 10 - 13.", "ITEMS 10-14.") follow an earlier Item's heading and stay, and a 10-K/A that amends only Part III still starts at its "Item 10." heading. Rows with "and" or "through" ("Part III – Items 10 through 14", "Items 10 and 11") are read as before, because a 10-K/A can open with such a heading ("ITEMS 7 AND 7A.").
26. **Unnumbered Item 1** (#23). In a 10-K or 10-KSB whose first Item found is Item 1A or Item 2, the first line after the last PART I line before that heading that holds only Item 1's standard title ("Business", or "Description of Business" in a 10-KSB) starts Item 1. It does not when an Item line comes between them, when the last PART line is another part, or when the line has other words or a page number. A Part I printed with no Item numbers at all has its first numbered heading in Part II and gets no Item 1. In the review, the rule was made to decline when the title line is printed more than once after the last Item line before the heading, as when every page of Item 1 prints "PART I" and "Business" as page headers: the last PART I line is then the header of Item 1's last page. Such a filing gets no Item 1, as before change 26.
27. **Contents rows by page number** (#20). An Item line that ends with a page number or a range of pages ("10 - 11", "10-11", "10–11", "F-3"), after a tab, two spaces or dot leaders, is a contents entry, whether or not a body heading repeats it. Going back from such a row, the Item line before it is one too when it is within 2,000 characters, the last line between them ends with a page number (the statements listed under "Item 1. Financial Statements" with their pages, or a title wrapped onto the line with the page), and it names an Item that comes earlier in the form than the row's Item, as the entries of a contents list do. The review added that last condition: without it, a body heading whose page ended with a table row and whose next page printed a running header of the same Item with a page number ("ITEM 7. MANAGEMENT'S DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS (Continued)   5"), or a heading followed by an index of Items after the body, was dropped. A row with a page number is never taken as the body copy of a heading, so an index of Items with pages before the signatures no longer replaces a short last body Item, and a 10-Q/A's explanatory note above a paged contents list pairs with the body headings.
28. **One-Item list before SIGNATURES** (#19). Two copies of an Item heading with the same title, with no other Item between them, are a contents entry and its body heading when the line after the first copy is a signatures heading, the second copy follows within 2,000 characters with no signature sentence before it, and signatures come after the second copy. This is an 8-K cover that lists its one Item and "SIGNATURES" just above the body. An Item with no text of its own just before the report's signatures, and an exhibit that repeats its heading, do not pass these tests.
29. **Cross-references that name a document** (#15). An Item line whose title, current or earlier, whole or by its first three words, goes straight on with "of" or "in", then "this", "our", "its" or a company name with "'s", and then names a report, a Form 10-K or a Form 10-Q ("Item 1A. Risk Factors, of our Annual Report on Form 10-K for 2019 describes ...") is not a heading when a heading of the same Item with the same title follows it before any heading of a later Item. Without such a heading after it, the line may be a run-in heading ("Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of the Company's 1998 Annual Report is incorporated herein by reference.") and stays a candidate. Change 19 covers such lines only when the line above ends inside a sentence.
30. **Power of attorney** (#29). A line that holds only "POWER OF ATTORNEY" or "POWERS OF ATTORNEY" (with or without a colon, starting with a capital letter) after the last Item's start ends the last Item, when the text after it, before the signatures, reads as a power of attorney ("KNOW ALL PERSONS BY THESE PRESENTS", "constitutes and appoints", "hereby authorizes", "attorney-in-fact", "whose signature appears below"). The review added that text test. A power of attorney or glossary line next to an exhibit number alone is an exhibit's description in a list printed cell by cell, not a heading. Exhibit numbers include "24", "24.1 *", "99.1*", "10(a)", "101.INS", "Exhibit 24", "EX-24", "24-1", "Exhibit 3-A" and "Exhibit 10-F-1", and a marker cell ("*", "†", "X") between the number and the description is looked past; the review widened these from plain numbers.
31. **Reports after Item 9B** (#25). 10-K Item 9B and 10-KSB Item 8B end at an auditors' report heading or a "Management's (Annual) Report on Internal Control ..." heading printed after them. Item 9A keeps its reports, and so does 10-Q Part II Item 5. Up to a period, colon, dash, bracket or tab, the words after the report's opening words must be capitalized, joining words, or words of such a title, also when the title wraps onto the next line. So a sentence that starts with the title ("Management's report on internal control over financial reporting is included in Item 9A") does not end the Item; the review added this test.
32. **Glossaries after the exhibits Item** (#24). When no Item follows it, the exhibits Item (10-K Item 15, the old Item 14, 10-KSB Item 13) ends at a glossary heading, as Item 16 does since change 18. The exhibit-cell test of change 30 applies to these glossary lines and to those of change 18.
33. **Statements after the last exhibits Item, with any page numbers** (#12). When no Item follows the exhibits Item and the statements Item (10-K Item 8, 10-KSB Item 7) only refers to the statements (it prints no block heading), the exhibits Item ends where statements printed after all of its own text begin, whatever their page numbers. That needs an auditors' report heading, then headings that repeat the titles of at least three lines printed before it in the Item (its list of the statements, or an index to them), and none of the Item's own captions or exhibit table headers after the report. The Item ends at an index heading printed between its own text and the report, or else at the report. Unlike change 17, an exhibit index printed after these statements does not keep them in the Item.
    - From the review: the Item's own captions and table headers now include "(a)(3). Exhibits.", "(b) Exhibit Listing", "(3) INDEX TO EXHIBITS", "Item 15(a)(3) Exhibits", "EXHIBITS.", "B) REPORTS ON FORM 8-K", "Exhibit" alone or followed by a tab, "Exhibit Description" and "Exhibit Title", for change 17's F-paged statements too. Exhibit rows with no caption or header do not count, because the notes and tables of the statements print lines of the same shape. The index must come after the Item's first line of text, with none of the Item's captions between it and the report, so an index printed as the Item's first text, its list of the statements, stays in the Item. The titles are looked up by prefix, which made the search linear in the Item's length; the reviewers' 1.7 to 3.0 MB inputs went from 1 to 17 seconds to under 0.1 seconds, with the same output.
34. **Page headers above a cut** (#26). Where an Item ends at an auditors' report, a statement index, a report on internal control or a glossary, up to three lines printed just above that heading go with the text after it, such as the company name at the top of each statement page. Each must have a letter, be at most 100 characters long, be printed again on a line of its own at least twice in the text cut off, more often there than before the cut, and not be the Item's first line of text. The text cut off runs up to the next Item or the end of the last Item's text. The first line of text comes after the heading line, the title line of a bare heading ("ITEM 9B." then "OTHER INFORMATION") and lines that hold only words of the Item's current or earlier titles. Header lines below a line that the text cut off prints again, but not often enough to be a header itself, continue a list and stay, such as the last entry of the Item's list of statements ("Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements") when the notes repeat it at the top of each page. The review added the last three conditions; the "more often" condition came from Energy Transfer's 2025 10-K in the sample, whose Item 9 would have lost "None.". Cuts at an attached document (change 4) do not move headers.
35. **Earlier Item titles** (#27). Each Item also lists the titles it had in earlier versions of its form: 10-K Item 4 "Submission of Matters to a Vote of Security Holders" and "(Removed and Reserved)", Item 6 "Selected Financial Data", the shorter titles of Items 5, 10, 12 and 13, Item 14 "Controls and Procedures", the old exhibits title of Items 14 to 16, 10-Q Part II Items 2, 4 and 6, the renamed 10-KSB and 10-QSB Items (10-QSB Part II Item 2 "Changes in Securities and Use of Proceeds" and "Changes in Securities"), and 8-K Item 5.02 of 2004 to 2006. A section's `title` is the current or earlier title that its printed heading names. The printed title must contain the title's first word, and the title with the most words in common, in order, wins. On a tie, the title with fewer words left unprinted wins: a title printed whole, or else the shorter, earlier one. An earlier title needs at least half of its words, and more than half when the heading shares a word with the current title, so a 10-Q's "Item 2. Issuer Purchases of Equity Securities" keeps the current title. A heading continues over up to three following lines while all of their words but at most one, and most of them, are words of the title. When nothing fits, the current title stays. The review changed the tie rule (the current title had won ties), the more-than-half condition, the one-word tolerance and the 10-QSB title. Earlier titles are never used to find headings, so ids, offsets and text do not change, and no score changes.
36. **Page numbers that end pages** (#16). A page number alone on its line in parentheses or with a period ("(12)", "12.") is removed when it is the last line before a printed page break and five or more such lines mostly come one to three pages apart. Page breaks are read from page-break and break CSS styles in HTML, also inside the cells of layout tables, and from `<PAGE>` tags in plain-text filings. Footnote markers over their footnotes and note numbers over a note's title look the same but do not end pages, so they stay. This changes no score.
37. **Contents rows only among other rows** (#36). Since change 27, any Item line that ends with a page number was a contents row, so a body heading printed with a footnote's number in a cell of its own ("Item 7.<TAB>Management's Discussion and Analysis ...<TAB>1") was dropped. Such a line is now a row only among other rows: another Item's line follows within 2,000 characters, or the Item line before it is that close and the last line above it, blank lines and lines holding only a Part aside, ends with a page too, or the next line does ("Signatures<TAB>5").
    - From the review: a contents list with one Item row, as in an amendment of one Item, is still contents. A lone row that prints a page before the body pairs with the next line of its Item when that line prints the row's whole title and no page. A running header that prints its page ("ITEM 7. MANAGEMENT'S DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS (Continued)   25") had stopped being a row, and could take the place of the body heading when another Item's line stood between them; it is a row again when an earlier line of its Item is not one and the header shortens the title or adds "(Continued)".
38. **Cross-reference indexes** (#32). The page column of a contents row may list several pages or ranges, F-pages included ("3-30, 64"). Inside a run of rows it may also say "cover", "N/A", "Not applicable", "None" or "*". A row's entry runs from its Item line to the next Item line, with the wrapped title or the pages listed under it, and lines holding only a Part are skipped. Rows are now found going forward from a row as well as back: the Item line just after a row is a row too when it names a later Item, another Item's line follows closely and its entry ends with a page column. The compliance date for the period is not asked, since filers adopt a new Item early: Pharma-Bio Serv's 10-K for the year that ended in October 2023 has an Item 1C on cybersecurity and keeps the title. Splitting without dates gives the titles it gave before.

## Other tools

`bench/external/run.sh` runs edgar-crawler and edgartools on the same filings and scores their output:

```sh
export PARSEC_EDGAR_USER_AGENT="Your Name you@example.com"
bench/external/run.sh            # development, held-out and blind sets
bench/external/run.sh fresh4     # one set or several: dev, holdout, blind, fresh, fresh2, fresh3, fresh4
```

It needs git, Python 3.11 for edgar-crawler and Python 3.10 or later for edgartools. Use `PYTHON311` and `PYTHON` to choose the interpreters. edgar-crawler pins numpy 1.24 and pandas 1.5, which have no wheels for later Pythons. The script stops when `PARSEC_EDGAR_USER_AGENT` is not set, and edgartools is given the same identity.

The script does the following:
1. Clones edgar-crawler at a fixed commit (84a8d0c, July 2025) and installs both tools (edgartools 5.62.1), each in its own virtual environment.
2. Exports the labeled filings with `export --full-submission`.
3. Runs each tool and writes its Items as one JSON file per filing.
4. Scores the output with `score-external`.

Clones, environments, exported filings and results stay in `bench/external` and are ignored by git. edgar-crawler is GPL-3.0, so `crawler_run.py` runs its `extract_items.py` as a separate program, and none of its code is in this repository.

- **edgar-crawler** reads the primary document, or the complete submission for filings without one, as its own downloader saves it. It runs with its default settings, including table removal.
- **edgartools** reads the complete submission through `Filing.from_sgml_text`, which is what it downloads for a filing. Its Items come from the report object's public API (`report.items`, then `report[item]`).
  - The network is blocked, so it never reaches the SEC.
  - For some filings, edgartools looks up the filing's index page, and the block stops that request with the error "network access is disabled in this benchmark". In the latest runs, of the development set, fresh2, fresh3 and fresh4, this happened on one development filing, Science Dynamics' 10-KSB 1998, on the 4 fresh2 filings from before 2001, on the 5 fresh3 filings from 2000 and before, all plain text (Max & Erma's Restaurants 1996, Red Brick Systems 1997, Cellular Communications of Puerto Rico's 10-Q 1998, Banknorth Group 1999 and the 10-QSB 2000 of Diamondhead Casino, filed as Europa Cruises), and on the 6 fresh4 filings from 2000 and before, all plain text too (Foamex International's 10-Q 1997, Medical Manager 1997, Photoelectron 1998, Aware 1999, Spectrum Laboratories' 10-KSB 1999 and BancFirst 2000). Earlier runs saw it on 8 development filings from 1997 to 2000 (seven plain-text filings and Walmart's 2000 10-K, which is HTML) and on the 4 plain-text filings of the fresh set; run with network access on those 12, edgartools made one or two requests per filing and returned no Items, so the score was the same either way.
  - 10-K405 and 10-KSB filings are read as 10-Ks, and 10-QSB filings as 10-Qs, because edgartools' 10-K and 10-Q classes accept only their own form and its amendment. edgar-crawler is given them as 10-Ks and 10-Qs too. Before the 10-QSBs were relabeled, edgartools stopped on all five of them with "AssertionError: This form should be a 10-Q but was 10QSB"; with the relabeling it finds 1 of the 26 Items of the three development 10-QSBs and none of the two in fresh3 or the two in fresh4.

The other tools' scores on fresh4 were measured with main. Those on the development set and on fresh3 were measured with main before changes 37 to 49, and those on fresh2 with main before changes 22 to 36. Those on the held-out, blind and fresh sets were measured before version 0.1.1 was released. None of the older runs was repeated. The labels and the filings of those sets are the same; what changed is this library's text, in which `score-external` places their sections (changes 5, 14, 20 and 49; change 49 removed page labels from eleven filings of the six older sets).

### Placing another tool's text

`score-external` finds each section's text in our filing text, comparing letters and digits only. A section starts at the line where its opening characters appear and the following text agrees best, which tells a body heading from its table-of-contents entry. Formatting conventions that do not move an Item's start are not counted as errors:
- a Part line or page header before the heading;
- a heading left out of the section text;
- a page footer inside the text;
- a body too short to find, such as "None.", when it equals the labeled Item's body.

`predict DIR` writes this extractor's own sections in the same format. Scoring them through `score-external` gives the same scores as scoring them directly, except span 0.998 against the direct 1.000 on the development set and 0.982 against 0.983 on fresh4. The six older sets were compared this way before changes 37 to 49, and fresh4 with main. A section that holds only its heading line can be placed at a later line with the same words this way, so `score-external` can score a wrong start as a hit; at fresh2's first scoring it did so for Superior Well Services' Item 7.01.

How to read these numbers:
- **Tuning.** parsec-edgar was tuned on the six older sets: on the development set while its rules were written, and on the others by fixes after their first scorings. The other tools were not tuned on any set. Compare the tools on the first scorings of the fresh4, fresh3, fresh2, fresh, blind and held-out sets. The development set has no such score.
- **edgar-crawler** finds Item starts nearly as well, from a hard-coded list of each form's Items. On fresh4 its F1 and its span accuracy are both lower than parsec-edgar's. At fresh3's first scoring its F1 was the higher one, because of one cross-reference 10-K, and at fresh2's its span accuracy was (above).
  - It loses span accuracy on the last Item, which runs on into attached annual reports (span 0.136 on the `annual-report-attached` filings).
  - Some 10-Q Items start at the table of contents or at a "(continued)" page header.
- **edgartools** returns no Items for most filings without an HTML document, so it scores 0.047 F1 on the 21 `plain-text` filings of the development, held-out and blind sets. It also misses Items in many exhibit-13 and small-company 10-Ks, in the 10-KSBs, and in the 10-QSBs, where it finds 1 of 26 Items.
- **Time.** The Python times include reading the filing from disk, and edgartools' times include parsing the whole submission. The parsec-edgar times start after the cached document has been read.
  - edgar-crawler's time is its whole run, minus its start-up time, divided by the number of filings.
  - edgartools' time is measured per filing.
